**Path:** `c:\clients\willdev\dbf\ORDHEAD.DBF` (81,793 records)

ORDHEAD = the "order header" table — every Load and every Deal is an ORDHEAD row. Relevant fields for sales attribution:

## 3. The Critical Distinction — ordhead vs tordhead

From `pilotbird:--facet-aload-sload` (verified):

- **`ordhead.dbf` + `ordtail.dbf`** (in `.\dbf\`) = **Accounting's books**. GL, AR, AP. App alias: `i_aload`.
- **`tordhead.dbf` + `tordtail.dbf`** (in `.\loads\X\LOADNUM\`) = **Sales workspace**. Where salespeople build and rearrange plans. App alias: `i_sload`.

**This is THE central fact about salespeople in ProduceFlow.** Salespeople live in tordhead. Their work doesn't touch ordhead until **Pre-Post** (which marks `on_hold=true` on ordhead) and finalizes at **Post** (which removes `on_hold`).

### Load Lifecycle

1. **Create load** → ordhead.dbf gets `abc=' '` record; `.\loads\X\LOADNUM\` created
2. **Sales works** → tordhead gets buyers/shippers/freight edits; ordhead unchanged for financial entries
3. **Pre-Post** → financial entries pushed from `.\loads\` to `.\dbf\`, marked `on_hold=true`
4. **Post** → accounting verifies, removes `on_hold`. Affects GL/AR/AP.

### Divergence (a real thing)

After Post, the two filing systems can diverge:
- Accounting adds: commissions, payment lines, supplier invoice numbers, price adjustments (ordhead only)
- Sales adds: new shippers, freight changes (tordhead only)

**Accounting (ordhead) is "more real," but sales (tordhead) is where the full picture lives.**

## 4. Deal Types — What Salespeople Build

Loads contain Deals (ABC-coded sub-records). Each deal type has a color and a role:

| Color  | Type         | Headpos | Financial? | Who Owns It              |
|--------|--------------|---------|------------|--------------------------|
| Green  | Buyer        | b       | Yes        | Salesperson sources demand |
| Red    | Shipper      | s       | Yes        | Salesperson sources supply |
| Blue   | Freight      | f       | Yes        | Salesperson books transport |
| Gray   | Broker       | k       | Yes        | Commission party          |
| Purple | Inventory    | i       | No         | Always in both ordhead+tordhead immediately |
| Orange | Store Req    | q       | No         | Always in both ordhead+tordhead immediately |

Purple and Orange are mutually exclusive (a load is either receiving inventory or releasing it).

## 6. The Salesperson as Workflow Actor

From `BUSINESS_CONTEXT/General ProduceFLOW Information.md` — Willis Produce example:

```
1. Order Receipt:   Publix orders → Willis enters order
2. Load Planning:   Willis sets delivery date/city
3. Sourcing:        Willis identifies Farmwey (Arcadia, FL)
4. Logistics:       Willis calculates freight time
5. Scheduling:      Willis works backward from delivery
6. Booking:         Willis books Farmwey purchase + Acme freight
7. Execution:       Tracked through PowerFLOW boards
```

The salesperson is the **load coordinator** — they're the human who:
- Receives orders from buyers
- Sources from shippers
- Books freight
- Sets dates
- Hands off to logistics/dispatch

Their workspace is `tordhead`. Their tool is the sales screen (salesgrid.js). Their accountability shows up in ORDHEAD.SALESMAN.
